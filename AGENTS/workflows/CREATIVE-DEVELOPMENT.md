# Creative development

Read this workflow whenever a consequential creative decision remains open during commercial preparation or refinement. It owns discovering, comparing, and strengthening the commercial's idea. [COMMERCIAL-WORKFLOW.md](COMMERCIAL-WORKFLOW.md) owns the five preparation steps, the single commercial record, assets, prompts, and handoff. [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) owns authorization and stops; [INVESTIGATIONS.md](../docs/INVESTIGATIONS.md) owns factual grounding; [VERIFICATIONS.md](../docs/VERIFICATIONS.md#creative-work) owns checks.

The user supplies what they know about the business and reacts to concrete proposals. The agent supplies creative direction, evaluates the possibilities, explains its recommendation, and develops the selection. Do not require the user to arrive with a concept or act as the creative evaluator.

## Enter at the unresolved decision

Read the request, relevant brand/product material, current commercial record, and supplied references. Identify what is settled, what is open or explicitly reopened, and which decision the requested work actually needs. Discover and compare ideas when direction is open; refine or develop directly when enough direction is established. A supplied direction, resumed commercial, or single-shot correction does not require a new round of concepts. Correct the lowest responsible layer first; a framing problem does not itself reopen strategy.

Use the process below only as far as the authorized outcome requires. A concept comparison can finish at a recommendation and selection question. A selected concept can enter at development. This workflow connects the brief to the script and supports later refinement; it is not a sixth preparation step or a mandatory full brand exercise.

## Find the communication opportunity

Use available evidence to understand the offering, relevant audience desire or problem, context of viewing, and the brand's credible contribution. Propose what this commercial should make that audience understand, feel, or do, and why that response matters. An audience insight is a hypothesis unless supported by research or supplied evidence; label proposed strategy separately from established facts and unknowns.

Ask only for missing information that materially changes the next decision. Explain why it matters and whether the answer is invited or required for dependent work. Offer a concrete proposed audience or communication opportunity when the evidence permits it instead of handing the user a blank strategy questionnaire. Research a specific unresolved question when it can improve the decision; do not make broad research a prerequisite to every idea.

Do not invent product advantages, customer research, testimonials, performance, or competitor weaknesses to make an idea persuasive. Keep concepts conditional where a material claim needs confirmation, identify its source or owner, and continue alternatives that do not depend on that claim.

## Develop the central idea

Turn the communication opportunity into something that happens on screen. Describe the premise and the audience's reason to watch: an observation they recognize, a question they want answered, an action that reveals value, or another specific effect. A mood, slogan, attractive product shot, or list of effects alone does not establish the idea.

Explore approaches suited to this business and audience: demonstration, human observation, metaphor, surprise, humor, emotion, spectacle, or other appropriate forms. These are possibilities, not required categories. A quiet sustained observation or precise demonstration can be as effective as a dramatic transformation. Do not impose a house genre, narrative formula, compulsory twist, voiceover, or constant spectacle.

Make the product or brand integral to the action, meaning, or recognition. Use its actual qualities and established identity where they help; visual metaphor must remain distinguishable from literal product behavior.

## Compare meaningful concepts

When direction is genuinely open, normally present two or three viable, meaningfully different ideas. Honor a request for one recommendation or a narrower correction. Vary the underlying premise, audience appeal, or product role; recolors, camera variants, and different adjectives for the same montage do not count as different concepts. Strengthen or replace weak candidates before presenting them rather than supplying obvious losers to favor one option.

Make each option concrete and comparable, in plain language:

- **Premise:** what happens and what it communicates.
- **Opening moment:** the first thing the viewer sees or hears and the reason to stay.
- **Progression:** the short sequence or sustained action that develops the idea.
- **Product/brand role:** why this offering belongs in this idea.
- **Payoff:** what the ending makes clear, felt, or actionable.
- **Production implications:** required assets, performance or continuity demands, sound intentions where material, feasibility, and unresolved dependencies.

These describe an option, not mandatory plot beats or a presentation template for every response. Use enough detail to judge the choice without fully scripting every candidate. Compare options at similar fidelity so polish or length does not decide by default.

## Critique and recommend

Evaluate the options against the [quality criteria](#quality-criteria), revise weak points, then recommend the strongest fit for the intended response and actual constraints. Explain the decisive reason, its tradeoff, and when another option would serve better. Do not leave the user with an unranked menu or ask them to perform the critique.

Invite a selection or reaction to the concrete recommendation. When a consequential preference is unresolved, obtain the needed choice before dependent development; continue independent authorized work while waiting. Existing selection or explicit delegation to choose is sufficient authority to proceed within scope. A recommendation, silence, or praise alone is not a selection. Do not ask again when context already establishes the choice.

## Develop the selection

Translate the selected premise into script, visual direction, pacing, sound intentions, and storyboard under the commercial workflow. Define execution principles that guide actual decisions: what attracts attention, when the product becomes legible, which visual relationships recur, where to hold or cut, and how the ending delivers the intended response. Translate labels such as "premium" or "energetic" into concrete behavior appropriate to this idea. Sound may be music, dialogue, voiceover, effects, ambient sound, or intentional silence; choose it for the effect rather than adding every layer.

Check the sequence as a whole, not just a hero frame. Use written beats initially; use visual boards or timing studies only when needed for the decision and authorized. Carry asset and production constraints into the existing storyboard, reference, and prompt preparation. If execution weakens the premise, revise the responsible shot, pacing, or treatment; explain and resolve a concept change only when the problem requires it. Preserve settled decisions and requested stops without introducing repeated approval gates.

Keep relevant audience insight, concept options, recommendation and tradeoffs, selected premise, and execution principles in the [single commercial record](COMMERCIAL-WORKFLOW.md#one-working-record-per-commercial) when preservation is needed. Distinguish proposals from selections. Retain rejected options only when useful to explain the decision or prevent repetition. No separate mandatory brand or creative record is introduced.

## Learn from references

For each important reference, identify the relevant principle, why it serves this communication problem, whether to adapt or reject it, and how this commercial will differ. Transfer a relationship such as delayed reveal, contrast in scale, sound-led anticipation, or product-led action rather than copying the reference's cast, set, genre, or sequence by default. Established brand decisions continue to govern.

Inspect the actual relevant material before attributing a technique to it. Sampled video frames support observations about those frames; they do not establish full pacing, dialogue, music, or sound design. State unassessed properties. A supplied video is an illustrative example unless the user explicitly selects a particular treatment; it never becomes a workspace-wide house style merely by being supplied.

## Quality criteria

Use these questions to make and explain judgments about both concepts and their execution:

- Is there a specific idea beyond attractive imagery and promotional copy?
- Does the audience have a reason to care or remain curious?
- Does the product or brand contribute meaningfully to the idea?
- Do the sequence, pacing, and ending deliver the intended effect?
- Could a competitor replace the logo without materially changing the commercial? If so, examine whether greater specificity is needed, without inventing uniqueness or claims.
- Do visual and audio choices strengthen the idea, including restraint or intentional silence where appropriate?
- Is it achievable with available assets and production methods without inventing product claims?

These are judgment criteria, not required plot beats, numerical creativity scores, or a demand that every work be unprecedented. Identify concrete weaknesses and revise them where possible; retain material tradeoffs and untested assumptions. Apply only the relevant questions to a narrow correction. Creative effectiveness remains unproven until actual work is evaluated; instruction wording and document checks alone cannot establish it.
